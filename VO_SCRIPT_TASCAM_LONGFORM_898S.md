**Language:** English only. No burned-in captions — a placeholder audio slot is reserved on the timeline.

### Tone (Stage 9)

**Phases 1–2: The Systems Architect.** Calculated and methodical, prioritising structural
logic and acoustic physics over feature listing. Chosen because the first two-thirds of the
film argue from signal flow — latency, phase coherence, noise floor, the three-way split —
and that is precisely the register Stage 9 describes for this direction.

**Phases 3–4: The Integration Authority.** The film's last third moves off the physics of a
single console and onto ecosystem mastery: grafting a digital engine onto a legacy desk, and
the systems expertise required to deploy it. Stage 9's Integration Authority sample is written
about exactly that — centralised synchronisation and workflow across a hybrid room.

**Delivery notes.** Roughly 140 words per minute. Full stops are real stops. Every number is
read as an engineer would say it aloud — "minus one hundred and twenty-eight dBu", "twenty-four
bit, forty-eight kilohertz". No rising inflection, no persuasion, no warmth for its own sake.
Read as a specification being presented, not a product being sold.

### Content rules observed throughout

- **Zero pricing.** No amounts, no currency, no commercial framing, anywhere in this script.
- **No comparison to any other audio brand.** The argument is entirely internal to TASCAM.
- **Every figure traces to a Stage 8 master table.** Nothing approximated, nothing rounded.
- The Model 16's fader travel is marked UNVERIFIED in Stage 8 and is **never stated**.
- The Studio Bridge is **never** described as participating in the Tri-Path Architecture.
- The Model 16 and Model 24 are **never** credited with MIDI, MTC or DAW transport.

## PHASE 1 — THE PROBLEM (00:00 – 02:00)

**[00:00 – 00:12] · Cold open · "THE ROUND TRIP"**
> Every hybrid studio runs the same loop. Analog in. Conversion. Processing. Conversion again.
> Analog out.

**[00:12 – 00:26] · "LATENCY IS NOT AN INCONVENIENCE"**
> Each stage costs time. And time, in a monitor path, is not a delay. It is a phase
> relationship that no longer agrees with the source it came from.

**[00:26 – 00:42] · "MIXED IN A BOX"**
> There is a second cost, and it is ergonomic. A pointer resolves one parameter at a time.
> A console resolves as many as the engineer has fingers.

**[00:42 – 01:00] · "TWO ROOMS"**
> So the work splits. Outboard analog on one side of the room. A digital timeline on the
> other. Bringing them together has historically meant committing to one of them.

**[01:00 – 01:16] · "ONE COMPROMISE"**
> A tactile desk that cannot capture multitrack. Or a multitrack interface with nothing to
> put a hand on.

**[01:16 – 01:34] · "WHY CHOOSE AT ALL"**
> The assumption underneath that compromise is that a signal has one destination. It does not.
> Split it at the point of amplification, before conversion, and it can have three.

**[01:34 – 02:00] · "FOUR CONSOLES. ONE ARCHITECTURE."**
> That is the architecture underneath the TASCAM Model series. Four consoles, at four
> different scales, built on one signal-flow decision. And a fifth unit that reaches the same
> destination by an entirely different route.

## PHASE 2 — THE TRI-PATH SOLUTION (02:00 – 11:20)

**[02:00 – 02:10] · "THE TRI-PATH ARCHITECTURE"**
> Model twelve. Model sixteen. Model twenty-four. Model twenty-four hundred.

**[02:10 – 02:22] · "ONE SIGNAL"**
> It begins with one source, arriving at one connector.

**[02:22 – 02:36] · "ULTRA-HDDA"**
> The signal meets an Ultra-HDDA preamplifier — High Definition Discrete Architecture. This
> stage is where the decision is made.

**[02:36 – 03:04] · Tri-Path Splitter graphic**
> Here the path fractures. One stream continues down the physical channel strip to the fader
> and the analog summing bus — no conversion, so no round trip, and no latency in the monitor
> path. A second dives into the solid-state recorder, writing twenty-four bit, forty-eight
> kilohertz audio to SD media with no host computer involved. A third becomes a USB data
> stream for the workstation.

**[03:04 – 03:16] · "−128 dBu EIN"**
> The noise floor beneath all three: minus one hundred and twenty-eight dBu equivalent input
> noise, measured at one hundred and fifty ohms source impedance, at maximum gain.

**[03:16 – 03:30] · "ANALOG · SD · USB"**
> The three happen at once. The engineer is never choosing between a live mixer, a field
> recorder and a studio interface. All three states are simultaneous.

## PHASE 3 — THE TRANSPARENT GRAFT (11:20 – 13:50)

**[11:20 – 11:34] · "NOT A FIFTH CONSOLE"**
> The Studio Bridge is not a fifth console. It has no preamplifiers, no faders, no channel
> strips and no summing bus. It does not participate in the Tri-Path Architecture, because it
> has no amplification stage to split a signal from.

**[11:34 – 11:48] · "STUDIO BRIDGE"**
> It is a twenty-four in, twenty-four out line-level bridge — built for an analog desk that
> already exists.

**[11:48 – 12:04] · "NO PREAMPS"**
> The omission is the specification. Direct line-level analog-to-digital conversion means the
> impedance and harmonic character of the engineer's existing front end reach the converter
> unaltered.

**[12:04 – 12:16] · "LINE LEVEL, BOTH WAYS"**
> Twenty-four channels out to the desk. Twenty-four back. The same level, the same standard.

**[12:16 – 12:44] · DB25 Injection graphic**
> Twenty-four lanes of line-level audio converge on high-density DB25 connectors, compliant
> with AES fifty-nine, two thousand and twelve. Inside the housing there is no preamplifier, no
> equaliser and no summing bus — so the response through it is flat and uncoloured, and what
> leaves over USB is what the desk sent.

**[12:44 – 12:58] · "24 × 24 DB25"**
> Six multi-pin connectors, in place of forty-eight discrete cables.

**[12:58 – 13:16] · Verified specification**
> Total harmonic distortion plus noise, at or below zero point zero zero three percent at one
> kilohertz. Dynamic range, one hundred decibels or better. Input impedance ten kilohms or
> greater; output, two hundred ohms.

**[13:16 – 13:34] · "WITH OR WITHOUT A DAW"**
> The same twenty-four track engine serves a hosted session, a computer-free capture, or an
> outboard preamplifier rack feeding it directly.

**[13:34 – 13:50] · "MIDI · MTC · SPP"**
> With MIDI in and out, time code, Song Position Pointer, and HUI and MCU transport control —
> from a six-U rack, rather than from a control surface.
