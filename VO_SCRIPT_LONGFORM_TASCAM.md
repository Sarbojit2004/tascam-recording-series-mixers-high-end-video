## Tone — which of the brief's options, and why

The creative brief's Section 9 offers three directions: Warm & Trustworthy (The
Mentor), Precise & Technical-but-Accessible (The Chief Engineer), and
Cinematic & Aspirational (The Visionary). This script runs primarily as
**The Chief Engineer**, with **The Mentor's** warmth at the seams — the cold
open, the chapter transitions, and the outro.

The reasoning: five minutes is long enough to actually teach the range, not
just gesture at it. A viewer staying this long wants the real numbers —
input counts, preamp counts, fader throw, subgroup counts — stated plainly
and correctly, which is the Chief Engineer's whole register. But a video that
is *only* specifications for five minutes reads as a spec sheet, not a story.
So every chapter opens and closes on the Mentor's more human framing — why
this product exists, who reaches for it — and the middle of each chapter
carries the hard numbers. This mirrors the reel's own choice (Chief Engineer
spine, Visionary bookends) adapted for a format with room to actually explain
rather than just gesture.

**Delivery:** measured, confident, unhurried — this is a specialist walking a
customer through the range in person, not reading ad copy. Let pauses land
between chapters; the picture is doing a lot of the pacing work, the voice
doesn't need to rush to keep up. Land every spec number cleanly.

**Pronunciation:** identical to the reel's guide — see below.
