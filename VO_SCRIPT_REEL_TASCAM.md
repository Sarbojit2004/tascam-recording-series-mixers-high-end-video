## Tone — which of the brief's options, and why

The creative brief's Section 9 offers three directions. This script uses
**Precise & Technical-but-Accessible (The Chief Engineer) as the spine, opened
and closed in Cinematic & Aspirational (The Visionary).**

The reason is the format. This reel moves 109 assets through 88 seconds with a
cut roughly every second — the picture is already doing the persuading, and a
Warm & Trustworthy read over that pace would fight it. The Chief Engineer voice
is economical: it names the number and gets out of the way, which is what a
fast montage needs from narration. But an engineer's read cannot *open* a reel
in a scroll feed, and it cannot close a sale — so the first two blocks and the
last two are Visionary, and everything between them is specification stated
plainly.

**Delivery:** steady, unhurried, low-mid register. Do not chase the cuts — the
picture is fast enough on its own; the voice should feel like the calm thing in
the room. Land hard consonants on the numbers. The words per block are budgeted
so every block ends with air before the next one starts; use it.

## Constraints observed in this script

- **No comparison with any brand other than TASCAM**, stated or implied.
- **No "distributor", "dealer" or "reseller"** — Shivansh Electronics is named
  as TASCAM's **authorized partner**, which is a designation, not an
  intermediary role.
- **No region-limiting language.** Availability is framed nationally: "best
  price across India".
- **Every specification is one the creative brief's Section 4 master table
  marks VERIFIED.** Nothing flagged UNVERIFIED is spoken. Pricing is the
  confirmed figure set, which supersedes the brief's own pricing flag.
- **Studio Bridge is never called a mixer.** Its block is built on subtraction
  — "not the fifth mixer — the opposite" — matching the brief's Section 2
  identity requirement.
